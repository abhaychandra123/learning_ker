  Can a physics-based learned inverse jointly estimate eﬀective standoﬀ and reconstruct a mag-
  netic image at a speciﬁed reference plane, while generalizing across unseen source geometries,
  ﬁelds of view, pixel spacings, and noise conditions?

validity.
Primary target: near-plane magnetic ﬁelds. Current density is developed as an optional latent
physical representation; this choice can be revised after the dataset audit.

1 Start with a tractable research claim

1.1 The project you are actually building

Your long-term objective is reliable reconstruction of sharp QDM magnetic images under uncertain
measurement geometry. The ﬁrst paper should answer a bounded version: for a single planar
source layer, can joint standoﬀ inference improve reconstruction of the magnetic ﬁeld at a ﬁxed
reference plane, including on source geometries excluded from training? FOV, sampling, and noise
robustness become explicit challenge sets. Multilayer separation and optical inversion are later
extensions with diﬀerent identiﬁability assumptions.
You have access to an extensive existing COMSOL database, GPUs, real QDM measurements,
and the experimental dataset underlying Bathla et al. [17]. Use these resources as the core of
the study. The few geometries visible in the current repository do not describe the full database
available to your group. This guide has not inspected that external database: counts, units,
available components, calibrated heights, and acquisition labels must be established by the audit
in Section 3.
  A one-sentence working claim
  We investigate a joint calibration-and-reconstruction method that uses analytical propagation
  and a learned source prior to estimate eﬀective standoﬀ and reconstruct a near-plane magnetic
  ﬁeld, with source-level holdouts and calibrated treatment of ambiguous observations.



1.2 Four hypotheses to investigate separately

1. H1: calibration matters. Height errors explain a measurable part of reconstruction error
   after boundary and sampling eﬀects are controlled.
2. H2: joint inference helps. Reﬁning image and distance together improves over a height
   predictor followed by a ﬁxed reconstruction network under matched training data and compute.
3. H3: ambiguity should be represented. A distribution or set of height–image hypotheses
   yields fewer conﬁdently incorrect decisions than a single estimate.
4. H4: physical metadata helps transfer. Explicit observation operators and physical pixel
   spacing improve transfer across FOV and sampling changes.

H1 can be rejected. H2 can be rejected. A simple baseline winning is useful information. Choose
one central methodological claim for the paper; treat the other hypotheses as controls or supporting
studies.

2 Physics, observations, and identiﬁability

2.1 Deﬁne the output before deﬁning the network

Let h be the sensor height relative to a declared source plane, and let zref be the ﬁxed target-plane
height. Initially use zref = 0.5 µm to match the repository. The propagation gap is δ = h − zref > 0.
Let x denote the vector ﬁeld at that plane on a larger physical domain, before restricting it to the
requested output ROI. The observation is

                                    y = CSQAδ x + η = Mh x + η.                                       (1)

Here Q projects onto measured NV axes or selects vector components; S integrates/resamples
to detector pixels; C restricts the observation window; and η denotes noise. Known optical and
NV-depth averaging can eventually enter Mh . They should be accounted for as nuisance eﬀects
when material, even while optical deconvolution remains outside the ﬁrst paper.
For simulation inputs, y can contain all three signed components. The published Bathla acquisition
uses a single NV-axis projection. A model must support the actual available channels; a single-axis
image cannot be relabelled as three independently measured components. The primary target is
a magnetic ﬁeld, not an optical photograph or binary wire segmentation.

2.3 An exact reason that residual-only height estimation fails

In an ideal inﬁnite-plane, noiseless model, suppose y = Aδ x. For any 0 ≤ δ ′ ≤ δ, deﬁne x′ = Aδ−δ′ x.
The semigroup property gives
                                               Aδ′ x′ = Aδ′ Aδ−δ′ x = Aδ x = y.                                               (4)
Thus a smoother reference ﬁeld at a smaller gap explains the same observation exactly. A ﬂexible
unconstrained ﬁeld can trade source structure against distance. A network trained on one coil size
may learn that size as a shortcut and call larger coils “farther away.”
Restrictions to real source families, geometric knowledge, controlled drive currents, and indepen-
dent calibration can make height inference informative. A learned estimate is conditional on those
assumptions. Physical consistency and extra vector channels improve practical constraints but do
not guarantee unique absolute depth for unrestricted planar sources.

2.5 What additional heights actually provide

Measurements at several heights above all sources are related by upward continuation in the ideal
complete-plane source-free model. They do not independently determine an arbitrary multilayer
decomposition. In practice they can improve SNR, registration checks, noise characterization,
and ﬁnite-window constraints. Known stage increments give relative distances; absolute standoﬀ
remains a separate calibration question.

2.6 A minimal physically constrained source representation

For planar currents, a stream function g can deﬁne Jx = ∂y g and Jy = −∂x g, enforcing ∇ · J = 0
away from contacts. Predicting g and using Biot–Savart provides a source-aware prior, then the
ﬁeld at zref becomes the main output. Contacts and currents crossing the FOV require appropri-
ate source-domain extensions and boundary treatment. Field-ﬁrst and source-latent formulations
should be compared, not silently conﬂated.
  Scientiﬁc interpretation
  Standoﬀ deblurring restores detail conditional on a prior and measurement model. A ﬁner
  output grid is not evidence of ﬁner physical resolution. Deﬁne success through recoverable
  features, ﬁeld accuracy, calibration accuracy, and reliability on held-out sources.

Create a report showing height counts per geometry, pitch/FOV distributions, missing compo-

3.3 Reuse complete height sweeps without learning test-pair kernels

Where a geometry has ﬁelds at zref and several higher planes, use the lower ﬁeld as target and
each higher plane as an observation. The analytical operator is evaluated from physical spacing
and height. A held-out sharp ﬁeld must not be used to ﬁt that observation’s empirical kernel.
Compare direct COMSOL high-plane ﬁelds with analytical propagation of the low-plane ﬁeld.
This residual includes export interpolation, discretization, ﬁnite-window error, registration, and
any modelling mismatch. Investigate its structure before ascribing it to mesh noise. A bounded
empirical correction may be a later experiment, but its parameters must be ﬁtted on training/cal-
ibration data only, and tested on unused geometries or acquisition sessions.

3.5 Splitting protocol and leakage controls

First assign source identities to train, validation, calibration, and test groups; only then derive
heights, current rescalings, crops, and noise variants. The calibration group is needed only if
uncertainty calibration is included and must be distinct from the model-selection validation set.
All descendants of a geometry remain in the same group. Near-duplicates and variants from the
same template generator may require a higher-level family grouping.
Maintain two diﬀerent evaluations: new-instance interpolation within a geometry family, and
unseen-family transfer. A random split of conﬁgurations is not evidence of unseen geometry.
Separate height interpolation from extrapolation. Record exact withheld ranges and test them
once after selecting the model.
For a pilot, choose approximately 100 independent layouts if available, with a 60/20/20 source-level
train/validation/test split. This is a starting engineering conﬁguration, not a sample-size justiﬁ-
cation. If calibration is needed, reserve additional sources or repartition without examining the
locked test outcomes. Expand according to learning curves and precision of source-level conﬁdence
intervals.

3.6 Sampling, cropping, and normalization

Propagate on a larger domain and then apply pixel integration and FOV restriction. For coarser
pitch, antialias and integrate the ﬁeld; do not simply resize an image and declare a new physical
spacing. Upsampling labels does not create new measured information. If the target uses a diﬀerent
grid, make that output grid explicit and interpolate only through a documented observation model.
Use a ﬁxed scale estimated from training data or an observation-only scale q(y), and retain q as
physical metadata when amplitude carries information. Reconstruct in physical units for metrics.
If minimizing the existing half-squared loss plus an L1 penalty under x = qu, the normalized
coeﬃcient is λ/q. Do not normalize using held-out sharp targets. At zero or near-zero signal, use
a predetermined scale ﬂoor and ﬂag height as uninformative.
Rotating a ﬁeld image requires rotating its vector components as well as its spatial coordinates.
Under a planar rotation R, rotate (Bx , By ) consistently and transform the NV projection direction
for scalar observations. Scaling geometry changes physics and cannot be implemented by spatial
resizing alone. Reverse all signed components for current reversal under the linear magnetostatic
model.

5 The experiment ladder: investigate before inventing

Each experiment should have a short conﬁguration ﬁle, immutable split identiﬁer, source revision,
arrays, metrics, and a visual report. Work through E00–E05 ﬁrst. The remaining experiments are
selected by the observed failure, rather than executed as an indiscriminate architecture sweep.


5.1 E00: operator correctness and model discrepancy

Question. Does the observation operator represent the data well enough that a new network is
scientiﬁcally interpretable?
Procedure. Check Fourier-mode attenuation, identity at zero gap, nonnegative upward gaps,
padding/cropping, pixel integration, component projection, and transpose ordering. Verify
⟨Mh x, r⟩ = ⟨x, M∗h r⟩ on asymmetric grids and masks. Compare the autograd derivative in h with
centred ﬁnite diﬀerences on a small problem. Compare independent COMSOL high-plane ﬁelds
with propagated reference ﬁelds, using interior and full-FOV metrics.
Controls. Increase physical-domain size/padding; reﬁne one representative COMSOL mesh; com-

error changes with window size, do not call it solely simulation noise.
Output. Adjoint/gradient errors, component residual maps, residual spectra, interior-versus-

5.3 E02: intentionally wrong height

Question. How much does calibration error aﬀect reconstruction, and can residuals diagnose it?
Select low, medium, and high heights from the actual database and multiple independent layouts.
Sweep assumed height around each true value, subject to h > zref . For an observation at 3 µm,
an illustrative sweep is 2 to 4 µm with ﬁner samples near 3 µm. Reconstruct to the same declared
solver tolerance at each candidate. Reuse warm starts only if their eﬀect and timing are recorded.
Plot reconstruction error, whitened observation mismatch, regularization cost, and feature/proﬁle
error against assumed height. Repeat at zero noise and two speciﬁed physical noise levels. Show
cases where a good residual accompanies a poor reconstruction.
Interpretation. A broad residual valley is consistent with ambiguity; a narrow prior-dependent
objective minimum does not establish true height. A monotone preference for the lowest permitted
height may reﬂect the semigroup degeneracy. Use this experiment to set application-relevant error
tolerances rather than inventing a success threshold after seeing test data.


5.4 E03: source–height ambiguity and shortcut detection

Question. Which changes in source parameters can imitate a height change?
Vary source size, track width, turns, spacing, location, and current independently of height. Find
nearest observation pairs from diﬀerent layouts/heights using a noise-weighted distance. For Gaus-
sian noise a likelihood comparison uses the whitened separation Σ−1/2 (y1 − y2 ) ; interpret it

under the actual covariance and sampling, not raw pixel count. Verify lookalike cases visually and
using proﬁles.
Train a minimal height predictor. Evaluate it on held-out size ranges, current amplitudes, and
shape families. Compare physical units, observation-only normalization with retained amplitude,
and normalization that discards amplitude. Do not discard amplitude and then conclude that it
is irrelevant.
Output. An ambiguity gallery; height error by geometry/current subgroup; predictive spread

should remain ﬁxed for comparing later models.


5.5 E04: the two-stage baseline

Question. Is a simple height estimator followed by reconstruction suﬃcient?
Train a CNN encoder with global pooling and an MLP receiving physical metadata. Predict h
within the allowed training interval. Feed its estimate to a conditioned reconstructor trained with
known heights. Evaluate a second variant in which the reconstructor is trained using noisy height
estimates matching the predictor’s validation errors; otherwise a brittle two-stage baseline can
make joint inference look unfairly strong.
Compare height MAE, ﬁeld error, failure rate, and latency with the known-height counterparts.
Repeat with at least several model seeds once the pilot is stable. Use the same prior capacity and
training budget wherever feasible.


5.6 E05: joint ﬁeld–height reﬁnement

Question. Does feedback between calibration and reconstruction add value?
Start from the two-stage initialization, then perform a small trained number of physics-and-prior
reﬁnement stages. Supervise both height and reference ﬁeld on simulation data; include observation
consistency. Compare (a) frozen height, (b) gradient height updates, and (c) learned residual-based
height updates. Control total parameter count and inference compute, not only stage count.
Required ablations. Same reconstruction prior; identical metadata; same dataset; matched
height initialization; no-reﬁnement model trained end to end; and a larger two-stage model under
a similar compute budget. Log intermediate heights and errors to detect unstable oscillations or
drift toward the bound.
  Decision gate
  Advance to a novel mechanism only if there is a reproducible residual failure: ambiguity, noise
  ampliﬁcation, scale mismatch, boundary inconsistency, or simulation-to-real mismatch. If the
  strong two-stage model solves the task, develop a diﬀerent scientiﬁc claim instead of obscuring
  it with added modules.

5.9 E08: noise and acquisition robustness

Start with known white Gaussian noise as a controlled experiment, then add correlated noise,
spatially varying variance, oﬀsets, and drift estimated from real repetitions. Keep physical noise
level distinct from SNR; increasing height reduces signal even if instrument noise remains ﬁxed.

but does not by itself reveal frequencies removed by standoﬀ. Check shared calibration noise,
ﬁtting-induced spatial correlation, and drift before assuming conditional independence. QDsiM [20]
provides a noise-aware measurement simulation reference; adopting a simulator alone is not a
methodological novelty.

if independently labelled height pairs are available. Separate gains from registration/calibration
improvements from gains due to the new architecture.


5.11 E10: physical resolution and prior dependence

Deﬁne a controlled feature family: for example two nearby track contributions, a narrow gap, or
a small perturbation on a known source. Sweep separation and SNR at several heights. Deﬁne a
detection or proﬁle criterion before locking the test set; report false positives and conﬁdence, not
just visual sharpness. Compare ordinary interpolation to rule out claims based solely on increased
output pixels.
Perturb the learned prior with plausible out-of-distribution layouts. If features appear in the
reconstruction without support in the measurement, test whether the uncertainty mechanism ﬂags
them. A successful resolution claim must state the source family and measurement conditions
under which it holds.

6.9 A6: uncertainty-sensitive spectral reﬁnement

This is a hypothesis motivated by E02–E03, not a veriﬁed new method. For a gap uncertainty σh ,
deﬁne sensitivity s(f ) = 2π ∥f ∥ σh . A provisional gate might be w(f ) = 1/(1 + s(f )2 ), multiplied
into the Fourier transform of a physics-gradient correction. Alternatively learn a gate conditioned
on estimated spectral SNR, height distribution, and pixel spacing.
Test ﬁxed low-pass gates, gates from true height error as an oracle diagnostic, and gates from
predicted uncertainty. Gates can stabilize updates but also remove real detail. Derive their eﬀect on
signed spectra, test feature false negatives, and compare under matched compute. Cropping means
this frequency operation is an approximate reﬁnement/preconditioner, not an exact diagonalization
of the whole observation operator.
The candidate contribution is justiﬁed only if predicted uncertainty improves reliable detail recov-

6.10 A7: current-latent inference as a stronger physical prior

Predict a current stream function or parametrized conductor source, compute J, then use Biot–

near-plane ﬁeld. Where COMSOL supplies trusted J, add auxiliary current supervision as an
explicitly available training signal. Compare with a ﬁeld-ﬁrst network receiving the same data.
This representation reduces implausible independent-component outputs, but its assumptions may
fail for contacts, ﬁnite thickness, vertical vias, or unknown multilayer sources. Geometry masks
can be known metadata in one regime and unknown in another; report separate results. Recovering
a latent J that ﬁts the ﬁeld does not prove unique current recovery.

7.2 Curriculum and optimization

1. Verify the forward/adjoint and height derivatives on tiny arrays.
2. Train A2 with known height on a stratiﬁed pilot. Inspect gradients, sign/amplitude conservation,
   and per-component errors.
3. Train the height predictor independently. Log error by source size/current and compare simple
   spectral features.
4. Train A3 end to end, including realistic height perturbations. This becomes the principal simple

5. Initialize A4 from A3; enable bounded height reﬁnement. Start with modest stage count and

Adam or AdamW is a reasonable pilot optimizer; an illustrative initial learning-rate grid is 10−4 to

For learned updates, ﬁx the trained depth at inference unless extrapolation is itself evaluated.
Classical solvers use the repository’s optimality-based stopping criterion with their actual update
counts and stop reasons. Ground truth is used for reporting and supervised training, never for
classical stopping or per-test regularizer selection.


7.3 Losses to add only with a reason

Edge/proﬁle losses can prioritize narrow features but amplify target interpolation artifacts. Spec-
tral losses require a deﬁned physical frequency grid and a reliability mask. SSIM is an optional
supplementary metric; its data range and component treatment must be ﬁxed. A reconstruction
that looks plausible while changing amplitudes may score well perceptually and still be scientif-
ically wrong. Avoid an adversarial loss until ﬁeld amplitude, sign, and uncertainty are already
controlled.
For vector ﬁelds, a physically constrained latent potential/source may be stronger than an arbitrary
penalty on components. A two-dimensional ﬁnite-diﬀerence divergence of (Bx , By ) is not the full
three-dimensional condition ∇ · B = 0; the missing ∂z Bz matters. Enforce the appropriate source-
free Fourier relations or full forward model rather than an incorrect 2D surrogate.


7.4 Uncertainty: what to predict and how to check it

Distinguish measurement noise (aleatoric), source ambiguity, training uncertainty, and discrepancy
in the measurement model. A small ensemble can probe model variability, but it is not a guarantee
of Bayesian coverage. For a discrete height distribution, report cross-entropy or NLL, Brier score
or reliability bins, height set coverage, and set width. For continuous estimates use calibrated
intervals, coverage versus nominal conﬁdence, and sharpness.
Conformal calibration [13] provides marginal coverage under its exchangeability assumptions. Hold-
ing out an entirely diﬀerent source family or instrument can violate those assumptions. Report
subgroup coverage under these shifts rather than implying a distribution-free guarantee still holds.
Calibration and model selection must use separate data or a justiﬁed cross-validation scheme.
Deﬁne a selective decision: accept a single-height reconstruction only when a calibrated height
set and candidate ﬁelds are suﬃciently concentrated under a criterion ﬁxed on calibration data.
Otherwise return the candidate set or label the result ambiguous. Evaluate the error–coverage
curve as more diﬃcult cases are rejected. Rejection must not remove all useful scenes.

8 Experimental validation with the real microscope

8.1 Plan the ﬁrst campaign around one falsiﬁable claim

The ﬁrst campaign should ask whether an unknown-height reconstruction improves a registered
closer-plane prediction for sources excluded from ﬁtting/adaptation. Start with a few indepen-
dently identiﬁable coil layouts, several sensor positions, repeated acquisitions, and at least two
acquisition sessions if practical. Scale the number after a pilot establishes repeatability and uncer-
tainty; a suggested count is an engineering starting point, not a statistical power calculation.
For the archived multilayer array, use individual-coil activations to begin with a single active source
and the corresponding background subtraction. Check whether routing, contacts, or wire bonds
introduce sources between reference and sensor planes; simple source-free upward continuation is
valid only above all relevant sources. A known layer depth can parameterize hℓ = d0 + zℓ , but the
common oﬀset d0 still needs calibration or explicit inference.


8.2 A measurement protocol

1. Select a known layout and measure actual drive current, including sign and possible heating
   dependence. Record the source reference surface and relevant layer thicknesses.
2. Record a reference/background acquisition with the same optics, microwave settings, and sensor
   conﬁguration.
3. Acquire the closest usable plane, followed by several larger relative gaps. Record stage displace-
   ment, approach direction, mechanical repeatability, and drift.
4. At each position acquire repetitions or retain raw traces suﬃcient to estimate ﬁtting noise and
   covariance. Keep timestamps and acquisition order.
5. Repeat a subset at another session and on withheld layouts. Fit registration/calibration using
   designated calibration acquisitions, not every test target.
6. Construct registered observation–reference pairs only after checking coordinate transforms, mag-
   niﬁcation, projection axes, ﬁeld units, and averaging.

8.3 What counts as height ground truth

A calibrated stage increment establishes a relative displacement. It does not independently deter-
mine NV implantation depth, physical contact gap, surface spacer thickness, or sample tilt. Report
absolute height only where the complete reference is independently characterized, with error bars.
Otherwise evaluate relative-gap estimation and near-plane prediction, and describe ﬁtted absolute
distance as model-dependent.
The magnetization study [18] is a useful conceptual reference for joint calibration with a physical
prior, but its material model is not a coil prior. Its source-dependent calibration illustrates why
nominal, model-ﬁtted, and independently measured distances belong in separate columns of the
experimental report.


8.4 Near-plane reference versus optical-free ﬁeld

If the closest measured image retains the optical PSF and NV-depth averaging, then it is a target
for standoﬀ improvement under the same instrument, not automatically the true unblurred ﬁeld
at 0.5 µm. Matching optics across height pairs helps isolate magnetic propagation, but changes
in focus, magniﬁcation, and averaging must be logged. COMSOL near-plane truth is a separate
evidence tier.
For the ﬁrst paper, report both simulated ﬁeld-truth accuracy and experimental closer-plane mea-
surement accuracy. Do not present the latter as complete optical deconvolution. If the physical
zref cannot be measured directly, state that restriction and validate the accessible target plane.


8.5 Domain adaptation without target leakage

Use an adaptation split of real coils/sessions. Fit low-dimensional nuisance calibration ﬁrst: regis-
tration, axis, gain, and relevant averaging. Then evaluate whether adapting the learned prior adds
value beyond that calibration. Record all test-time optimization steps and include them in timing.
Repeated noisy acquisitions can train a denoising consistency term if the source is unchanged and
noise assumptions are supported. Unsupervised repropagation constrains the measurement but
leaves the joint height–source ambiguity; combine it with validated priors or calibration information.
A model trained against its own reconstruction cannot establish independent sharp-ﬁeld accuracy.


8.6 Multilayer extension and useful acquisition design

When source drive is controllable, temporal coding can produce genuinely useful independent
source information. Known, distinguishable excitation waveforms let you demodulate components
before reconstructing each contribution. Design codes to improve conditioning under measured
noise and acquisition budget, rather than merely collecting more redundant height planes. Test
cross-talk and synchronization error. This can be a later co-design contribution, but dynamic
source ﬁtting itself already exists in the group dataset.

A.2 A diﬀerentiable propagation fragment

This example handles a symmetric padded ﬁeld operator only. Projection, pixel integration, un-
equal latent/observation support, masks, and whitening must be composed separately with their
correct adjoints. It demonstrates that the transfer is computed from a tensor height without
converting it to NumPy or detaching it.




                                               27
QDM research playbook                                              Joint calibration and reconstruction


import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def propagate(x, h, dx_um, z_ref_um=0.5, pad=32):
    # x: [batch, channels, H, W], signed fields
    # h: [batch], in um, kept in the autograd graph
    # dx_um: common physical pitch for this batch
    H0, W0 = x.shape[-2:]
    xp = F.pad(x, (pad, pad, pad, pad)) if pad else x
    ny, nx = xp.shape[-2:]
    fy = torch.fft.fftfreq(ny, d=dx_um,
                           device=x.device, dtype=x.dtype)
    fx = torch.fft.fftfreq(nx, d=dx_um,
                           device=x.device, dtype=x.dtype)
    freq = torch.sqrt(fy[:, None]**2 + fx[None, :]**2)
    gap = (h - z_ref_um).reshape(-1, 1, 1, 1)
    transfer = torch.exp(-2 * torch.pi * gap * freq)
    out = torch.fft.ifft2(torch.fft.fft2(xp) * transfer).real
    return out[..., pad:pad + H0, pad:pad + W0]

# Validate positive gaps and pitch in the caller.
# Cache frequency grids by shape/pitch/device/dtype, not H(h).
# Different dx and dy require separate frequency spacings.

For the same real radial transfer and symmetric support, the adjoint has the same pad/con-
volve/crop form. That statement does not extend automatically to arbitrary C, S, or Q; their
adjoints must insert residuals, transpose averaging, and transpose projection in reverse order.
Check the implemented operator rather than assuming a transpose from its diagram.

1. Days 1–2: inventory the existing COMSOL database; deﬁne source identities, height origin,
   units, and available targets. Request the experimental archive and logs.
2. Days 3–4: freeze a pilot split; validate analytical propagation, adjoints, and height derivatives
   against small cases and several COMSOL pairs.
3. Days 5–6: produce known-height classical reconstructions and the wrong-height sweep. Examine
   boundary and spectral errors.
4. Days 7–8: form source–height lookalike pairs; vary source size/current independently. Write
   the ambiguity diagnosis.
5. Days 9–10: train a small known-height learned baseline or height predictor, depending on the
   diagnosis; draft the one-page next-experiment argument.

B.5 A ﬁnal claim audit

Before submission, ask whether the novelty is an algorithm, an uncertainty method, an observation
model, or an experimental capability. State what was given at inference and what was estimated.
Describe which ﬁne features were validated independently, which depend on priors, and whether the
height reference is absolute or relative. Make “unseen geometry” mean excluded physical identities,
not new crops of the same coil. Make “super-resolution” mean a deﬁned physical detail-recovery
result, not a larger tensor.


C Focused reading and questions for collaborators

C.1 Read with an implementation target

1. LISTA and ALISTA: derive the layer from a proximal iteration and reproduce initialization
   equivalence [1, 2].
2. U-Net and FiLM: build a multiscale signed-ﬁeld baseline, then test a single metadata-
   conditioning change [3, 4].
3. MoDL/VarNet: implement exact data consistency for this observation model instead of copy-
   ing the MRI encoding [5, 6].
4. QDM current reconstruction: identify the actual observation components, priors, and stand-
   oﬀ/noise comparisons [15, 16, 14].
5. Bathla dictionary work: reproduce the available-data task and distinguish coeﬃcient sparsity
   from ﬁeld-pixel sparsity [17].

6. Joint calibration and Bayesian source inference: map which assumptions stabilize the
   inverse [18, 19, 11].
7. Calibration: implement coverage and selective-risk evaluation before adding a more complex
   posterior network [12, 13].


C.2 Questions to resolve when the hard disk arrives

• Which maps are raw measurements, ﬁtted ﬁelds, averaged references, or dictionary reconstruc-
  tions?
• Are all original basis maps and activation/drive labels available, and are actual currents
  recorded?
• Are there repeated acquisitions, current-oﬀ data, raw ﬁt residuals, and timestamps?
• Were heights independently calibrated, or estimated by a model? Which coordinate is the
  origin?
• Which coils and layer depths are known, and which CAD variants have corresponding COMSOL
  models?
• Do the archive’s measurements include a usable closer-plane reference, or should we acquire
  one?
• Which arrays/sessions can be withheld for adaptation, calibration, and ﬁnal testing?

These questions are operational metadata requirements. They do not require changing the agreed
primary target or delaying the simulation pilot.
